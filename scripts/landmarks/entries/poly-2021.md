## What happened

On 10 August 2021 Poly Network, which runs a bridge for moving tokens between blockchains, announced that it had been attacked on Ethereum, BNB Chain and Polygon, and listed the addresses that received the funds [1].

Over the following days the attacker returned the funds. On 23 August Poly Network said all user assets had been recovered, apart from USDT that had been frozen [2].

## What the chain shows

The ether moved inside a contract call, so its amount is read from the trace; the two stablecoins moved as ordinary token transfers. This page shows these three because it can price them from exchange records; Merkle Science lists the other tokens taken alongside them [3].

## Why it matters

A bridge holds pools of tokens for several chains at once, so a single attack could take funds on all three chains together [1]. The attacker's handover ended with an on-chain message publishing a key to the wallet where the remaining funds sat [2].

It is also a case where a freeze shaped the outcome: the USDT taken here stayed frozen, and Poly Network counted it apart from the assets it recovered [2].

## What happened next

Poly Network, which called the attacker Mr. White Hat, wrote on 23 August that it had regained control of the affected assets and was returning them to users [2].
