## What happened

On 27 September 2021 a user's deposit of 100,000 USDT to DeversiFi, a trading platform built on Ethereum, carried a fee of 7,676 ETH [1]. DeversiFi blamed a flaw in a widely used Ethereum software library that met the network's new fee rules, and said the user's hardware wallet may have shown the fee in a form that was hard to read [1].

The sending address carries an Etherscan tag naming Bitfinex; DeversiFi's account calls the depositor a user [1].

## Why it matters

Since Ethereum's London upgrade the base part of a fee is burned, but the tip goes to whoever produces the block, and nothing in the protocol caps it [1]. A wrong number is paid in full, and only the recipient's goodwill can return it.

Here the miner of the block received the fee [1]. A deposit worth a small fraction of the fee became, by value, a transfer to a stranger.

## What happened next

DeversiFi found the miner through Binance, where the miner deposited mined ether, and the miner returned 7,626 ETH that evening, keeping 50 ETH that DeversiFi had offered as a fee for the return [1]. DeversiFi said it added a ceiling on transaction fees to its own software [1].
