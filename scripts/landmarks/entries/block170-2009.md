## What happened

In March 2013 Hal Finney, a cryptographer who had run Bitcoin from its first release, wrote a forum post about his part in its early days [1]. He wrote that he grabbed the software as soon as it was announced, and that he was the recipient of the first bitcoin transaction, ten coins sent to him as a test [1].

Finney wrote the post after years of illness: by then he operated his computer with an eye tracker, and he described still writing code that way [1].

## Why it matters

Until this block every coin on the chain had come straight from mining. This payment is where the ledger started recording transfers, and its receiver tied himself to it in his own words [1].

The chain itself records only two public keys. Finney names himself as the receiver; his post is the only account of who sent the coins, so the sender stays unnamed here [1].

## What happened next

Finney wrote that as the price climbed he moved his coins to an offline wallet, where he hoped they would be worth something to his heirs [1].
